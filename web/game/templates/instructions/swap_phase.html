Setup

The first thing you should do is hit the “settings” button and enter your email address. This is the email we will be using to communicate with you and send you compensation. After providing your email and once you are ready to participate in our user study, please hit the “Next” button. We show where both of these buttons are below:

After hitting "Next", you will be given some time to read the question. Afterwards, you will be asked to answer the question while following one of these plans. We provide details on both of these steps below.

Step 1: Read the Question

The first thing you will do is read the question for 60 seconds. You will not be allowed to interact with the rest of the interface until this time is up. You can also press the "Skip" button if you are ready to move onto the question:

Step 2: Answering Questions

In the next step, you will be asked to follow a plan of step-by-step instructions to answer the question. Specifically, you will see one step at a time and will be asked to compute the intermediate result of each step while having access to either a Calculator or a version of Google search. You will also be able to swap between two different plans if you find one of them not helpful. Once you reach the end of the instructions, you will provide a final answer. The first question will also be a tutorial question that will not count towards your progress to get you familiar with the interface.

a. Following the Plan

When a question is loaded, the interface will be divided into two halves: the left half will contain a question and a plan for you to follow, while the right half will contain tools to help you answer the question. You will initially see just one step of the plan and will be asked to store the answer to this instruction. Once you are happy with your answer, you can hit "Next Step" in the top left of the screen to see the next step in the plan. You will be able to see all the previous steps and modify all your previous answers.

Additionally, you will be able to swap between different plans by hitting the "Swap Plan" button in the top left corner of the screen. By hitting this button, you will see a different plan that can be used to help you answer the question. You can hit this button if you feel your current plan is unhelpful, or if you want to check out the other plan. Please note that when you swap for the first time, you will start over with a new plan, so you must decide when it is worth it to swap plans so you can still finish answering the question quickly. There are a total of two plans you can swap between, and your progress (intermediate answers) will be saved in case you want to switch back to your original plan. You can swap as many times as you like.

We display and point out all of these visual elements below:

If you hit "Next Step" on accident and want to undo seeing the next step, you can use ctrl/cmd+Z or hit the "X" in the top-right corner of the plan. If you do not want to follow the plan, you can hit "I will not follow any plan" and you will be asked to write your own plan to answer the question.

Please note that some of the plans will have attention checks with verbatim instructions for you to follow (e.g. "Type answer 'XYZ'", "Swap to the other plan"). If you do not pass these attention checks, we may deny compensation.

b. Using Tools

Questions are divided into two types: math questions or trivia questions. As a result, you may need external help when solving these questions. Instead of using other tools that would require you to navigate off our UI, we provide versions of calculators and Google search to help you answer the question.

For the calculator tool, you can manually write a math equation or press operation buttons with mathematical symbols (e.g. "+", "-", etc.) to build an equation in the upper input field. After hitting the calculator icon, the result will be stored in the bottom input field:

For the web search tool, there are two search fields. The top field allows you to find the most similar Wikipedia page based on a given query or keywords. Once you search here, you will see the Wikipedia page and a second search field you can use, which allows you to look for information inside the document (like ctrl/cmd+F). After entering a query, the page will scroll to the sentence that is most similar to the query and highlight the text. You can also scroll up and down the page to look for information:

All tool inputs have a "Clear" button which removes the text in the input field. There is also a "Copy" button that moves the current output in the calculator tool or the highlighted text on the page to the current step in the plan to easily store your answer. For the web search tool, you can also highlight any text on the Wikipedia page and press ctrl+C to copy that text on the page into the current step of the plan. Similarly, each step in the plan has a "Copy to Tool" button which takes the answer to the plan and adds it to the input field of the tool.

c. Providing a Final Answer

Once you reach the end of the plan, you will see buttons named "Buzz" that you can press to input your final answer to the question. The buttons are shown below:

Once you hit buzz, you will see an input field at the top of your screen where you can type in your answer to the question. You will have 8 seconds to answer the question and can hit "Enter" to submit your answer. By default, this input area will be filled with whatever is typed in the last step of the plan:

After buzzing, you will be told whether your answer is correct or incorrect. If the answer is correct, you can move on to the next question. If the answer is incorrect, you can keep trying to answer the question until you get it correct or time runs out. You will obtain lower "Question Accuracy" scores if you answer incorrectly (see Compensation section), so do not randomly guess. If you believe your answer is wrongly marked as correct/incorrect, or you want to give up on the question because it is too difficult, you can report the question (see section (e)).

d. Keyboard Shortcuts

We also provide keyboard shortcuts for every button in the app to help you answer questions quickly:

  • N: Start the next question or move onto the next step (depending on which button appears).
  • S: Swap plan, or skip the timer (depending on which button appears).
  • ctrl/cmd + Z: Undo the current step that was loaded.
  • M: Focus on the calculator tool input.
  • W: Focus on the web search tool input.
  • F: Focus on the search (i.e. ctrl+F) tool input.
  • C: Copy the current result in the calculator or highlighted text on the wiki page into the plan.
  • Enter: Run the tool (e.g., calculate your equation) when inside a tool text inputs or proceed to the next step/buzz with an answer when inside the step text inputs.
  • Space: Buzz to give your answer.
  • Tab: Open the settings pane.
  • Escape: Unfocus from your current text input.

All buttons or tools are labeled with these shortcuts to remind you. We encourage you to use these keyboard shortcuts to improve your speed.

e. Reporting Questions

If you believe there is an error or you want to move onto the next question, you can report the current question by hitting the "Report" button in the top left. This button will appear after 90 seconds to ensure that you gave a good-faith effort in answering the question:

After hitting this button, you will have to select one of the following reasons why you are reporting the question, or provide a textual description of your issue:

After reporting the question, you will be able to move onto the next question. Reported questions do not count as questions completed for the compensation below.

Compensation

You must answer at least 10 questions to be eligible for compensation. We will pay $5 for every 5 questions you answer. We will additionally give $50 to the 5 users with the highest average accuracy scores and $50 to the 5 users with the lowest time taken (i.e. fastest speed). You are eligible to obtain both $50 rewards. Reported questions are not included in this calculation.

More compensation details can be found on the incentives page. You can track your accuracy and speed scores on the leaderboard page.
