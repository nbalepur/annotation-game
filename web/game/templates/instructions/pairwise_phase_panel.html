Instructions

Thank you for participating in our user study! Below, we have instructions on how to participate and how you will be compensated. You can return to this page at any time by hitting the "Instructions" tab in the header of the website. We also have a tutorial video below which you can watch instead of reading all of the instructions. Before starting the user study, please either read the instructions or watch the video, and then click the button at the bottom of the page.

Setup

The first thing you should do is hit the “Settings” button to adjust the type of questions you would like to see along with your account details. After adjusting these settings and once you are ready to participate in our user study, please hit the “Next” button. We show where both of these buttons are below:

After hitting the "Settings" button, you will see a modal divided into three sections:

  1. Question Type (top): The Question Type panel allows you to choose the questions you would like to answer in the study. You can choose from Math (algebraic word problems using a calculator), Trivia (multi-step trivia questions using a search engine), and Everything (both math and trivia). We encourage you to choose Everything, as we have extra rewards for the top-performing users in each category
  2. Account (middle): The Account panel allows you to alter your email or username. The email linked to your account is what we will use to distribute rewards, so make sure you put the right email. The username is just for visual purposes and will be displayed on the Leaderboard
  3. Game Log (bottom): The Game Log shows your past interactions, like when you have joined the room, if you have answered questions correctly, etc.

After closing the settings and hitting "Next", the user study will begin. As a summary, you will be asked to select which of two step-by-step plans is better for an input question. Afterwards, you will be asked to answer the question while following one of these plans. We provide details on both of these steps below.

Step 1: Select which Plan is Better

The first thing you will see is a question and two diverse step-by-step plans that can be used to answer the question. Your goal is to select which plan you believe would help you answer the question more accurately and quickly (you can also determine that the plans are tied). Please note that the plan you select may or may not be the plan you interact with in the second stage. The interface for this comparison is shown below:

We will allocate you 60 seconds to complete this step. We will also have attention checks where one plan is intentionally low-quality, and we may deny compensation if you are found to fail these attention checks by selecting the low-quality plan.

Step 2: Answering Questions

As a high-level overview of the second step, you will be asked to follow a single plan of step-by-step instructions to answer the question. Specifically, you will see one step at a time, and will be asked to compute the intermediate result of each step while having access to either a Calculator or a version of Google search. Once you reach the end of the instructions, you will provide a final answer. The first question will also be an untimed tutorial question that will not count towards your progress to get you familiar with the interface. We will now demo each part of our interface.

a. Following the Plan

When a question is loaded, the interface will be divided into two halves: the left half will contain a question and a plan for you to follow, while the right half will contain tools to help you answer the question. You will initially see just one step of the plan and will be asked to store the answer to this instruction. Once you are happy with your answer, you can hit "Next Step" in the top left of the screen to see the next step in the plan. You will be able to see all the previous steps and modify all your previous answers. We display and point out all of these visual elements below:

If you hit "Next Step" on accident and want to undo seeing the next step, you can use ctrl/cmd+Z or hit the "X" in the top-right corner of the step. If you do not want to follow the plan, you can hit "I will not follow any plan" and you will be asked to write your own plan to answer the question.

Please note that some of the plans will have attention checks with verbatim instructions for you to follow (e.g. "Type answer 'XYZ'"). If you do not pass these attention checks, we may deny compensation.

b. Using Tools

Questions are divided into two types: math questions or trivia questions. As a result, you may need external help when solving these questions. Instead of using other tools that would require you to navigate off our UI, we provide versions of calculators and Google search to help you answer the question.

For the calculator tool, you can manually write a math equation or press operation buttons with mathematical symbols (e.g. "+", "-", etc.) to build an equation in the upper input field. After hitting the calculator icon or the Calculate button, the result will be stored in the bottom input field:

For the web search tool, there are two search fields. The top field allows you to find the most similar Wikipedia page based on a given query or keywords. Once you search here, you will see the Wikipedia page and a second search field you can use, which allows you to look for information inside the document (like ctrl/cmd+F). After entering a query, the page will scroll to the sentence that is most similar to the query and highlight the text. You can also scroll up and down the page to look for information. You can also click on hyperlinks on the Wikipedia page.

All tool inputs have a "Clear" button which removes the text in the input field. There is also a "Copy" button that moves the current output in the calculator tool or the highlighted text on the page to the current step in the plan to easily store your answer. Similarly, each step in the plan has a "Copy" button which takes the answer to the plan and adds it to the input field of the tool.

You are not required to use tools (e.g. if you know the answer to a trivia sub-question, or for simple calculations like "2+2" where calculators are not needed), but we encourage you to use them to reduce the chance of making a minor mistake that leads to an incorrect answer.

c. Providing a Final Answer

Once you reach the end of the plan, you will see buttons named "Buzz" that you can press to input your final answer to the question. The buttons are shown below:

Once you hit buzz, you will see an input field at the top of your screen where you can type in your answer to the question. You will have 5 seconds to answer the question and can hit "Enter" to submit your answer. By default, this input area will be filled with whatever is typed in the last step of the plan:

After buzzing, you will be told whether your answer is correct or incorrect. If the answer is correct, you can move on to the next question. If the answer is incorrect, you can keep trying to answer the question until you get it correct or time runs out. If you believe your answer is wrongly marked as correct/incorrect, or you want to give up on the question because it is too difficult, you can report the question (see section (e)).

d. Keyboard Shortcuts

We also provide keyboard shortcuts for every button in the app to help you answer questions quickly:

  • N: Start the next question or move onto the next step (depending on which button appears).
  • P: Focus on the next step in the plan.
  • ctrl/cmd + Z: Undo the current step that was loaded.
  • M: Focus on the calculator tool input.
  • W: Focus on the web search tool input.
  • F: Focus on the search (i.e. ctrl+F) tool input.
  • C: Copy the current result in the calculator or highlighted text on the wiki page into the plan.
  • Enter: Run the tool (e.g., calculate your equation) when inside a tool text inputs or proceed to the next step/buzz with an answer when inside the step text inputs.
  • [: Mark that the left plan (plan A) is better in the pairwise comparison.
  • ]: Mark that the right plan (plan B) is better in the pairwise comparison.
  • Space: Buzz to give your answer.
  • Tab: Open the settings pane.
  • Escape: Unfocus from your current text input.

All buttons or tools are labeled with these shortcuts to remind you. We encourage you to use these keyboard shortcuts to improve your speed. If the keyboard shortcuts are not working, you may need to refresh the page

e. Reporting Questions

If you believe there is an error or you want to move onto the next question, you can report the current question by hitting the "Report" button in the top left. This button will appear after 90 seconds to ensure that you gave a good-faith effort in answering the question:

After hitting this button, you will have to select one of the following reasons why you are reporting the question, or provide a textual description of your issue:

After reporting the question, you will be able to move onto the next question. Reported questions do not count as questions completed for the compensation below.

Compensation

You must answer at least 10 questions to be eligible for compensation. We will pay $5 for every 5 questions you answer. We will additionally give $50 to the 3 users with the highest average accuracy scores and $50 to the 3 users with the lowest time taken (i.e. fastest speed) across both question types (12 extra rewards total). You are eligible to obtain up to four of the $50 rewards. Reported questions are not included in this calculation. The time spent reading the plans in the beginning and the run time of tools does not count towards the calculation.

More compensation details can be found on the Incentives page. You can track your accuracy, speed scores, and number of questions finished on the Leaderboard page.
