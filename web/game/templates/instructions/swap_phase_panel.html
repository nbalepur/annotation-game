Instructions

Thank you for participating in our user study! Below, we have instructions on how to participate and how you will be compensated. You can return to this page at any time by hitting the "Instructions" tab in the header of the website. Before starting the user study, please watch the short tutorial video below, and then click the button at the bottom of the page. If you have any questions after watching the video, you can refer to specific sections below.

Settings

Before starting the next trial, you can hit “Settings” button to adjust the type of questions you would like to see along with your account preferences. After adjusting these settings and once you are ready to participate in our user study, please hit the “Next” button. We show where both of these buttons are below:

After hitting the "Settings" button, you will see a modal divided into three sections:

  1. Question Settings (top): The Question Settings panel allows you to choose the questions you would like to answer in the study. You can choose from Math (algebraic word problems using a calculator), Trivia (multi-step trivia questions using a search engine), and Everything (both math and trivia). We encourage you to choose Everything, as we have extra rewards for the top-performing users in each category. For trivia questions, you can also adjust if you would like the web page to auto-scroll to the sentence in the page most similar to your search query.
  2. Account Settings (middle): The Account Settings panel allows you to alter your email or username. The email linked to your account is what we will use to distribute rewards, so make sure you put the right email. The username is just for visual purposes and will be displayed on the Leaderboard
  3. Game Log (bottom): The Game Log shows your past interactions, like when you have joined the room, if you have answered questions correctly, etc.

After closing the settings and hitting "Next", the user study will begin. As a summary, you will first be given some time to read the question. Afterwards, you will be asked to answer the question while following one of these plans. We provide details on both of these steps below.

Step 1: Read the Question

The first thing you will do is read the question and plan for 30 seconds. You will not be allowed to interact with the rest of the interface until this time is up. You can also press the "Skip" button if you are ready to move onto the question:

Step 2: Answering Questions

As a high-level overview of the second step, you will be asked to follow a plan of step-by-step instructions to answer the question. Specifically, you will see one step at a time and will be asked to compute the intermediate result of each step while having access to either a Calculator or a version of Google search. You will also be able to swap between two different plans if you find one of them not helpful. Once you reach the end of the instructions, you will provide a final answer. The first question will also be an untimed tutorial question that will not count towards your progress to get you familiar with the interface. We will now demo each part of our interface.

a. Following the Plan

When a question is loaded, the interface will be divided into two halves: the left half will contain a question and a plan for you to follow, while the right half will contain tools to help you answer the question. You will initially see just one step of the plan and will be asked to store the answer to this instruction. Once you are happy with your answer, you can hit "Next Step" in the top left of the screen to see the next step in the plan. You will be able to see all the previous steps and modify all your previous answers.

Additionally, you will be able to swap between different plans by hitting the "Swap Plan" button in the top left corner of the screen. By hitting this button, you will see a different plan that can be used to help you answer the question. You can hit this button if you feel your current plan is unhelpful, or if you want to check out the other plan. Please note that when you swap for the first time, you will start over with a new plan, so you must decide when it is worth it to swap plans so you can still finish answering the question quickly. There are a total of two plans you can swap between, and your progress (intermediate answers) will be saved in case you want to switch back to your original plan. You can swap as many times as you like.

We display and point out all of these visual elements below:

If you hit "Next Step" on accident and want to undo seeing the next step, you can use ctrl/cmd+Z or hit the "X" in the top-right corner of the step. If you do not want to follow the plans because you believe they are both too low-quality, you can hit "I can't answer with the given plan" and you can either write your own plan to answer the question, or skip the question (but skipping the question will give you no credit).

Please note that some of the plans will have attention checks with verbatim instructions for you to follow (e.g. "Type answer 'XYZ'", "Swap to the other plan"). If you do not pass these attention checks, we may deny compensation.

b. Using Tools

Questions are divided into two types: math questions or trivia questions. As a result, you may need external help when solving these questions. Instead of using other tools that would require you to navigate off our UI, we provide versions of calculators and Google search to help you answer the question.

For the calculator tool, you can manually write a math equation or press operation buttons with mathematical symbols (e.g. "+", "-", etc.) to build an equation in the upper input field. After hitting the calculator icon or the Calculate button, the result will be stored in the bottom input field:

For the web search tool, there are two search fields. The top field allows you to find the most similar Wikipedia page based on a given query or keywords. Once you search here, you will see the Wikipedia page and a second search field you can use, which allows you to look for information inside the document (like ctrl/cmd+F). After entering a query, the page will scroll to the sentence that is most similar to the query and highlight the text. You can also scroll up and down the page to look for information. You can also click on hyperlinks on the Wikipedia page and navigate through your "search history" by pressing the left and right arrows.

All tool inputs have a "Clear" button which removes the text in the input field. There is also a "Copy" button that moves the current output in the calculator tool or the highlighted text on the page to the current step in the plan to easily store your answer. For the web search tool, you can also highlight any text on the Wikipedia page and press ctrl+C to copy that text on the page into the current step of the plan. Similarly, each step in the plan has a "Copy" button which takes the answer to the plan and adds it to the input field of the tool.

You are not required to use tools (e.g. if you know the answer to a trivia sub-question, or for simple calculations like "2+2" where calculators are not needed), but we encourage you to use them to reduce the chance of making a minor mistake that leads to an incorrect answer.

c. Providing a Final Answer

Once you reach the end of the plan, you will see a button named "Answer" that will take the input you have typed in the last step of the plan, and take that as your final answer. The button is shown below:

If you have chosen not to follow the plan, once you hit the "Answer" button, you will see an input field at the top of your screen where you can type in your answer to the question. You will have 8 seconds to answer the question and can hit "Enter" to submit your answer.

After submitting your answer, you will be told whether your answer is correct or incorrect. If the answer is correct, you can move on to the next question. If the answer is incorrect, you can keep trying to answer the question until you get it correct or time runs out. If you believe your answer is wrongly marked as correct/incorrect, or you want to give up on the question because it is too difficult, you can report the question (see section (e)) by hitting the button in the top left.

d. Keyboard Shortcuts

We also provide keyboard shortcuts for every button in the app to help you answer questions quickly:

  • N: Start the next question.
  • P: Focus on the next step in the plan.
  • S: Swap plan, or skip the timer (depending on which button appears).
  • ctrl/cmd + Z: Undo the current step that was loaded.
  • M: Focus on the calculator tool input.
  • W: Focus on the web search tool input.
  • F: Focus on the search (i.e. ctrl+F) tool input.
  • C: Copy the current result in the calculator or highlighted text on the wiki page into the plan.
  • Enter: When pressed outside of a text field, moves onto the next step or submits an answer. When inside a text field, runs the action associated with that field (e.g. calculate the equation)
  • ←: Move backward in the search history.
  • →: Move forward in the search history.
  • Tab: Open the settings pane.
  • Escape: Unfocus from your current text input.

Tool shortcuts do not work when your cursor is inside a text field. So if the shortcut is not working, please press Escape, which will first unfocus you from your text input.

All buttons or tools are labeled with these shortcuts to remind you. We encourage you to use these keyboard shortcuts to improve your speed. If the keyboard shortcuts are not working, you may need to refresh the page

e. Reporting Questions

If you believe there is an error or you want to move onto the next question, you can report the current question by hitting the "Report" button in the top left.

After reporting the question, you will be able to move onto the next question. Reported/skipped questions do not count as questions completed for the compensation below. The time spent in the pairwise comparison and the run time of tools does not count towards the calculation.

Compensation

You must answer at least 25 questions to be eligible for compensation. We will pay $5 for every 5 questions you answer. We will additionally give $50 to the 3 users with the highest average accuracy scores and $50 to the 3 users with the lowest time taken (i.e. fastest speed) across both question types (12 extra rewards total). You are eligible to obtain up to four of the $50 rewards. Reported/skipped questions are not included in this calculation. The time spent reading the plans in the beginning and the run time of tools does not count towards the calculation.

If you were recruited to this study from a University course in exchange for extra credit, your Professor will later set up an assignment on your course website where you can submit the email you used to complete the study. Once we verify you completed the study (answering 25 questions), your professor will distribute your extra credit accordingly. If you decide to complete our study and do not fill out the assignment in your class, you are still eligible for the monetary compensation above. If you answer more than 25 questions and choose coursework credit, the additional questions you answer are eligible for the monetary compensation above.

More compensation details can be found on the Incentives page. You can track your accuracy, speed scores, and number of questions finished on the Leaderboard page.
